How to play

Step on the beat

What's on the screen, how each step is judged, and how to get going, whether it's your first song or your hundredth.

Reading the screen

Arrows scroll up the screen toward a row of outlines at the top, the step zone. As each arrow reaches its outline, step on that arrow on your mat. The music tells you when; the arrows tell you where.

How steps are judged

Every step is judged by how far it lands from the beat. The closer it is, the better the judgment; outside every window it's a miss. Good steps in a row build your combo, and misses drain your life bar.

Themes name the windows differently. DDR-style themes say Marvelous, Perfect, Great and Good; ITG-style ones say Fantastic, Excellent, Great, Decent and Way Off.

For scale: a mat read every 8 ms adds up to ±4 ms of wobble to every step, a big share of the ±22.5 ms best window. That's why the Pi reads the mats every 1 ms. The game itself reads them once per frame, which adds up to ±8.3 ms at 60 fps, so a steady frame rate matters as much.

Your first session

  1. Switch on the receiver and the TV, then the Pi. OutFox starts by itself.
  2. The first time, OutFox asks which game type to play. Choose dance.
  3. Move through the menus with the arrows on your mat. Start chooses, Back goes back. Keep a USB keyboard nearby for settings and the sync keys.
  4. Pick a song, then a difficulty. Start on Beginner or Easy.
  5. Stand in the middle of the mat, listen for the beat, and step as each arrow meets its outline.

If every step seems early or late, the sync needs calibrating: build guide, step 10.

Difficulty

Most songs have several charts, from easiest to hardest: Beginner, Easy, Medium, Hard and Challenge. DDR calls them Beginner, Basic, Difficult, Expert and Challenge. Each chart also has a number, its rating; higher is harder.

Move up when you can clear a level without looking at your feet. Accuracy on easier charts builds the timing that hard charts need.

Two players

  • Versus: both play the same song side by side, each on their own mat and at their own difficulty. P1 is always the left mat.
  • Double: one player uses both mats as one eight-panel pad. The stage puts the two bays side by side, so Double works on it too.

Arrow speed

If arrows arrive too fast to read, or bunch up so you can't tell them apart, change the scroll speed in the player options before a song. A multiplier such as 1.5x or 2x scales the song's own speed. A constant speed (for example C400) keeps arrows moving at the same rate even when the song changes tempo.

Technique and comfort

  • At first, come back to the middle of the mat between steps. As songs get faster, alternate feet instead of returning each time.
  • Play in clean, soft-soled shoes or barefoot. Socks slip.
  • Keep the area around the stage clear, and keep water nearby.
  • The safety bar is for balance, not for hanging on.

Sync keys

On a keyboard plugged into the Pi, during a song:

KeysWhat they do
F6 twiceAutoSync Machine: OutFox adjusts the Global Offset from your steps for the rest of the song, then asks before saving.
Shift+F11 / Shift+F12Nudge the Global Offset, for every song (StepMania 5.1's keys).
F11 / F12Nudge this song's own offset only.

If every song feels off, recalibrate the Global Offset. If only one pack does, fix those songs' offsets and leave the global one alone.
