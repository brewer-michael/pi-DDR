Getting new songs

OutFox plays StepMania-style song packs. Here's how they're organised, where to find songs you're allowed to use, and how to get them onto the Pi.

How songs are packaged

A song is a folder holding a chart file (.ssc or .sm), the music (usually .ogg or .mp3) and, most of the time, a banner and a background image. Songs come in groups, usually called packs. On the Pi they go in OutFox's user folder:

~/.project-outfox/Songs/
└── Club Fantastic Season 1/        a group (pack)
    └── COOL_EXCEPTION/             a song
        ├── COOL_EXCEPTION.ssc      the charts
        ├── COOL_EXCEPTION.ogg      the music
        ├── COOL_EXCEPTION-bg.png   background
        └── COOL_EXCEPTION-bn.png   banner

The Songs folder next to the OutFox program works too. OutFox also reads DWI, SMA, BMS, DTX, .chart and KSF files, but dance charts nearly always come as .ssc or .sm.

Keep the two levels

Every song must sit inside a group folder. A song folder placed straight in Songs makes OutFox stop loading and point it out, and a group buried one folder too deep (a common result of unzipping) doesn't show up at all.

Where to find songs

OutFox Serenity

The OutFox team's own packs of freely redistributable music, with charts for dance and other game types. A few may already be bundled with the game. projectoutfox.com/outfox-serenity

Club Fantastic

Free seasons of original music made by the StepMania and In The Groove community, charted for dance. It's the example pack in OutFox's own getting-started guide.

Community archives

Sites such as StepMania Online and Zenius -I- vanisher host thousands of packs. Many of them use music ripped from commercial games, so check what you're allowed to download where you live.

Your own music

OutFox has a built-in chart editor, so you can chart songs you own. While you're charting, turn FastLoad off so the game notices your changes: stop the game over SSH with sudo systemctl stop getty@tty1, set FastLoad=0 in ~/.project-outfox/Save/Preferences.ini, and reboot.

Copy songs onto the Pi

Unzip the pack first. The folder you copy is the group folder, the one that contains the song folders.

From another computer: drag and drop

The pi-DDR installer shares the song folder on your home network. Open it with the host name and user name you set in Raspberry Pi Imager (here pi-ddr and player) and the share password the installer asked for:

  • Windows: type \\pi-ddr.local\Songs into File Explorer's address bar. To keep it, right-click This PC and choose Map network drive.
  • macOS: Finder, Go → Connect to Server, smb://pi-ddr.local/Songs.
  • Linux: smb://pi-ddr.local/Songs in the file manager.

Then drag the group folder in. Copying while someone is mid-song is fine, but the game has the Pi to itself if you copy between sessions. Forgot the password? Over SSH: sudo smbpasswd -a player.

From another computer: over SSH

ssh player@pi-ddr.local mkdir -p .project-outfox/Songs
scp -r "Club Fantastic Season 1" player@pi-ddr.local:.project-outfox/Songs/

From a USB stick

Raspberry Pi OS Lite doesn't mount sticks by itself. Over SSH, with the stick plugged into the Pi:

sudo mount /dev/sda1 /mnt
mkdir -p ~/.project-outfox/Songs
cp -r "/mnt/Club Fantastic Season 1" ~/.project-outfox/Songs/
sudo umount /mnt

Then reload

Quit OutFox from its menu. The pi-DDR setup starts it again straight away, and it picks up the new songs. If a song still doesn't appear, delete ~/.project-outfox/Cache and quit again. The next start rescans every song, which takes a while with a big library.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Space. Packs with background videos are big. df -h ~ shows how much room is left on the card.
  • Background videos. If a song with a video stutters, turn song backgrounds off in OutFox's options, or drop the game's resolution to 1280 × 720.
  • Whole packs off sync. If every song in one pack feels early or late by the same amount, the pack was synced differently. Packs made for In The Groove machines are often about 9 ms off from others. Fix those songs' offsets with F11/F12, not the Global Offset.

Song folder layout and formats are from OutFox's own guide; nothing here has been tried on the Pi yet. Corrections are welcome on GitHub.
